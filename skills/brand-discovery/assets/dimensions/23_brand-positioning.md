# Brand Positioning

An effective positioning will earn the brand a distinctive place in the mind of the audience as to why they are the most relevant choice.

## Audience Truth

Write this statement in language the customer would recognize.

> When [audience] tries to [goal], they run into [friction] because existing alternatives are built for [different priority], not [what this audience actually needs].

## Competitive Alternatives

What would this customer do if this brand didn't exist? The real set of options, including doing nothing.

| Alternatives | What it gets right | Where it falls short |
|---|---|---|
| Status quo / do nothing | [Why it's a real option] | [The cost of staying put] |

## Unique Value

What this brand does differently and what that difference makes possible for the customer. Shift from attribute to transformation.

| | |

## Market Category

How we frame the category matters; it sets the audience's expectations before they've read a word of copy.

> This brand is a [category framing], not a [common misframing]. The distinction matters because [how the framing changes perception and who it attracts].

## Positioning Statement

A synthesis of the sections above. The brief that all external copy should trace back to.

> For [specific audience] who want [outcome], [brand] is the [market category] that [unique value]. Unlike [alternatives], [brand] [primary differentiation], so [what becomes possible for the customer].
