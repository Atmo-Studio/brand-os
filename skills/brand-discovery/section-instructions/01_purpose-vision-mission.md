# Purpose, Vision & Mission: BRND-1
*Foundations, dimension 1 of 2*

**Fills:** Brand purpose, Vision, Mission
**Frameworks:** Golden Circle (Sinek), Core Ideology + BHAG (Collins & Porras) → `frameworks.md §1, §2`

---

## Failure Modes
- Founder gives a mission when asked for purpose: describes what they do, not why they exist
- Purpose is too generic: could belong to any brand in the category (substitution test fails)
- Vision is an operational target ("reach 10K customers") rather than a future state of the world
- Mission has no named audience or outcome: reads as a capability statement, not a commitment
- All three say the same thing in different words: no logical chain from purpose → vision → mission

## Interview Rounds

**Round 1: Purpose**
*Extract: The foundational belief driving the brand — why it needs to exist, not what it does.*

- Why did you start this? Not the business case: the real reason.
- What do you think is broken or undervalued in the world that this brand is trying to change?
- If this brand succeeded completely and then disappeared, what would be different: not for your company, for the people it touched?
- Who gets cheated or underserved if this brand doesn't exist?

*Technique, competing convictions:* If the founder's answer holds multiple distinct convictions, name them and ask which carries more weight. This often produces the clearest Purpose articulation and naturally separates Purpose from Mission.

*Advance when: the purpose names a conviction specific enough that a competitor's name couldn't replace the brand's.*

**Round 2: Vision**
*Extract: A future state the brand is working toward — aspirational, specific to this brand's worldview, not a business target.*

*If Vision material surfaced during Purpose rounds, reflect it back rather than re-asking: "You mentioned [X] earlier — is that the vision?" Confirm or refine.*

- What does the world look like when this brand has fully succeeded? Not revenue: what actually changes for the people you serve?
- What would have to be true about your industry for you to feel like you'd won?
- If someone wrote about this brand 25 years from now, what would the headline be?

*Advance when: the vision describes a changed world, not a growth milestone or a vague aspiration. If the founder is thinking too small, introduce the BHAG frame (frameworks.md §2).*

**Round 3: Mission**
*Extract: A specific commitment — action verb, named audience, concrete outcome.*

- What is this brand actually committing to do, for whom, toward what outcome?
- Who specifically are you doing this for?
- What are you promising them: not your features, the outcome they actually get?

*Advance when: a clear verb + specific audience + concrete outcome are all present.*

**Round 4: Coherence (conditional)**
*Run only if the three outputs pull in different directions or the mission feels disconnected from the purpose.*

- Does the mission feel like it's serving the purpose? If you achieved it completely, would you be closer to why you exist?
- Is there any tension between these three you want to name?

---

## Gap Check

Run all three internally. Surface the single strongest concern.

- [ ] Substitution test: replace the brand name with a competitor's: does the purpose still hold? If yes, it's not specific enough. *If a name origin story or founding philosophy surfaces here, fold it into the draft. This pressure often draws out the most ownable language.*
- [ ] Vision test: is the vision a genuine future state or a disguised operational goal / vague abstraction?
- [ ] Coherence test: does the mission directly serve the purpose? Does achieving it move the brand toward the vision?

`AskUserQuestion`
> "Before we lock this in — [state the strongest concern as a direct question]."
- Feels solid: write it
- Let me revisit [purpose / vision / mission]

## Fill Targets

| Field | Heading | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Brand purpose | `## Brand purpose` | `[Brand] exists to ______.` |
| Vision | `## Vision` | `To [aspirational future state].` |
| Mission | `## Mission` | `To [verb] [audience] [toward what outcome].` |

*Purpose: substitution test passes — competitor name breaks the statement.*
*Vision: a changed world, not a business target. Specific enough to mean something.*
*Mission: clear verb + named audience + concrete outcome. Short enough to remember.*
