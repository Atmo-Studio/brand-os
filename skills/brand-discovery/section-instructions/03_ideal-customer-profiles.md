# Ideal Customer Profiles · BRND-3
*Audience & Market · Page 1 of 3*

**Fills:** Up to 3 ICP toggle blocks (Context, Functional Job, Emotional Job, Social Job, Quote)
**Frameworks:** Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen et al., 2016) → `brand-strategy/frameworks.md §4`

---

## Failure Modes
- Describing demographics instead of jobs — "millennial professionals" is not a customer profile; a person with a specific struggle hiring a specific solution is
- Defining the job at the feature level ("they need a dashboard") instead of the outcome level ("they need to look competent in front of their board without spending a week on reporting")
- All ICPs sound the same — if the context, jobs, and quote are interchangeable between profiles, they're not distinct enough to be useful
- Ignoring the social job — often the most powerful purchase driver, and the one founders are least likely to articulate unprompted
- Writing aspirational ICPs (who the founder wishes they served) instead of actual ICPs (who they've already served successfully or who has already shown up organically)

## Interview Rounds

**Preamble — how many ICPs?**

Before Round 1, establish scope. Most brands have 1–3 meaningfully distinct customer profiles. More than 3 usually means the founder hasn't prioritized — or the distinctions aren't real.

`AskUserQuestion`
> "How many distinct types of customers do you serve? Defined by what they're trying to accomplish and why they come to you, rather than segments by size or industry"
- One primary type
- Two distinct types
- Three or more

*If three or more: push back once. "Can you describe how the third is different from the first two in what they're actually trying to get done?" If the distinction holds, proceed with three. If it collapses, work with two.*

*Run Rounds 1–3 for each ICP sequentially. Complete one profile before starting the next. Between profiles, briefly reflect what distinguishes the new ICP from the previous one.*

---

**Round 1 · Context & Functional Job**
*Extract: Who this person is, the situation they're in, and what they're literally trying to accomplish.*

- Tell me about a specific customer you've already worked with or who's already shown up. What's their role? What's their day-to-day like?
- What was happening in their world right before they came to you? What triggered the search? What broke, what changed, what got urgent?
- What are they literally trying to get done? What outcome are they working toward?
- If your brand disappeared tomorrow, what would this person do instead? What's the real alternative, including doing nothing?

**Technique**
*The switching moment:* The most useful ICP data comes from real switching stories. If the founder describes someone who actually hired them, ask: "What were they using before? What finally made them switch?" The push/pull dynamic (what pushed them away from the old solution + what pulled them toward this one) reveals the functional job more precisely than any hypothetical.

*Advance when: you can name a specific person (or composite), describe the situation they're in, state the functional job as an outcome (not a feature), and identify the real alternative.*

**Round 2 · Emotional & Social Jobs**
*Extract: How this person wants to feel when the job is done, and how they want to be perceived by others.*

- How does this person feel about themselves and their situation when they hire you and it goes well?
- What's the emotional weight they're carrying before they find you? What does relief look like for them?
- How does this person want to be seen by the people around them — their team, their clients, their peers, their boss? What reputation are they trying to protect or build?
- If this person's colleague asked "Why did you go with [brand]?" what would they want to be able to say? What would make them look smart for choosing you?

**Technique**
*The "brag test":* The social job often surfaces when you ask: "What would this person brag about to a peer after working with you — not about you, about themselves?" This separates the social job from the functional outcome.

*Advance when: the emotional job is a feeling (not a feature benefit), and the social job is about perception by others (not self-perception — that's emotional). If they're collapsing into the same answer, push: "The emotional job is how they feel privately. The social job is how they want others to see them. Those are often different — are they here?"*

**Round 3 · The Quote**
*Extract: A sentence that could have come from a real conversation with this person — captures the tension between where they are and where they want to be.*

- If this person were venting to a friend about the problem you solve — before they found you — what would they say? Give me the sentence, in their words.
- What's the thing they'd never say in a meeting but absolutely think?

**Technique**
*Founder ventriloquism:* Founders often speak for their customers in their own language, not the customer's. If the quote sounds like marketing copy, push: "That sounds like something you'd write about them. What would they actually say — probably less polished, maybe a little frustrated?" The best quotes are slightly raw.

*Advance when: the quote sounds like something a real person would say in conversation — not a testimonial, not a tagline. It should carry the emotional and functional tension of the job.*

**Round 4 · Distinction Check (conditional)**
*Run only if: multiple ICPs have been defined and the profiles feel too similar — the contexts overlap, the jobs sound interchangeable, or the quotes could belong to any of them.*

- If you had to choose only one of these profiles to serve — who would you pick and why?
- What's the one thing ICP [A] needs that ICP [B] doesn't care about at all?
- Would you talk to these two people differently in a sales conversation? What would change?

## Gap Check

Run all three internally. Surface the single strongest concern.

- [ ] Job-level test: is each functional job stated as an outcome the person is trying to achieve — not a feature they want or a demographic they belong to? Could a competitor's customer have the exact same functional job? If yes, the context or emotional/social dimensions need to be sharper.
- [ ] Distinction test: if you swapped the names on two ICPs, would anyone notice? If the profiles are interchangeable, they're not distinct enough to be useful — merge or sharpen.
- [ ] Real-person test: is each ICP grounded in someone the founder has actually served or spoken to — not an invented persona? If the founder can't name a specific person or story behind the profile, it's aspirational.

`AskUserQuestion`
> "Before we write this — [state the single strongest concern as a direct question, e.g., 'ICP 2 and ICP 3 have nearly identical functional jobs — the difference seems to be industry, not what they're trying to accomplish. Should we merge them and sharpen the distinction on ICP 1 vs. ICP 2?']"
- Looks solid — write it
- Let me revisit [ICP]

## Fill Targets

| Field | Notion section | Format |
|---|---|---|
| ICP toggle block | `### ICP [N] [Name / Role]` | Toggle heading with name or role descriptor |
| Context | `Callout: Context` | 2–4 sentences. Who they are, the situation they're in, what triggered the search. Written as a narrative, not a bullet list. |
| Functional Job | `Callout: Functional Job` | 1–2 sentences. What they're literally trying to accomplish — stated as an outcome, not a feature. |
| Emotional Job | `Callout: Emotional Job` | 1–2 sentences. How they want to feel when the job is done — a feeling, not a feature benefit. |
| Social Job | `Callout: Social Job` | 1–2 sentences. How they want to be perceived by others — reputation, status, or credibility they're trying to protect or build. |
| Quote | `Quote block` | One sentence in the customer's voice. Should sound like something said in conversation — slightly raw, carrying the tension of the job. Not a testimonial. |

*Context: specific enough that you could find this person on LinkedIn — not so specific it's only one human.*
*Functional Job: passes the "what would they do if you didn't exist?" test — the alternative is clear.*
*Emotional Job: a feeling, not a restatement of the functional outcome.*
*Social Job: about others' perception, not self-perception.*
*Quote: fails the "marketing copy" test — if it sounds polished, it's not real enough.*
